# learn-meditation-with-phoebe - source + coverage map

Secular beginner meditation on-ramp. 6 sessions, single track, running project = the learner's
own 4-week starter practice plan. Signature interactive: med-live.js breath pacer (real tool,
nothing simulated). Every substantive claim below was verified against the cited source
(research sweep 2026-08-04). The honesty rail: three claim types, always labeled -
research-supported (with actual numbers), "traditionally said to" (tradition), and marketing.

## Verified fact sheet (numbers exact - use as written)

### Evidence base (Session 1)
- Goyal et al 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine: 47 trials, 3,515 participants; 10 good / 24 fair / 13 poor quality. Vs nonspecific active controls at 8 weeks: anxiety SMD 0.38 (CI 0.12-0.64), depression SMD 0.30 (CI 0.00-0.59 - lower bound touches zero, do not call robust), pain SMD 0.33 (CI 0.03-0.62). Low/insufficient evidence that meditation beats exercise, progressive muscle relaxation, or CBT on ANY outcome.
- Van Dam et al 2018 "Mind the Hype", Perspectives on Psychological Science, 15 authors (mostly meditation researchers, not outside skeptics): definitional inconsistency, passive/waitlist controls inflate effects, underpowered samples, publication bias, unreported adverse events. Framed as fix-the-research, not stop-practicing.
- Kral et al 2018, NeuroImage: MBSR n=32 vs active control n=35; reduced amygdala reactivity to POSITIVE pictures only; the negative-stimuli effect appeared only in long-term meditators (~9,081 lifetime hours). "8 weeks calms your amygdala" is NOT what it showed.
- Kral et al 2022, Science Advances (Davidson's own lab): 218 meditation-naive participants (waitlist 70 / MBSR 75 / active control 73) - NO structural brain change from MBSR, failed to replicate prior findings. The strongest available correction to "meditation rewires your brain in 8 weeks".
- Basso et al 2019: 13 min/day guided meditation vs podcast control - benefits at 8 weeks but NOT at 4 weeks (attention, working memory, mood). Never shorten to "13 minutes works fast".

### MBSR reference points (Sessions 1, 3, 4)
- MBSR = 8 weekly classes of 2.5-3.5 hrs + one 7.5-hr silent all-day between weeks 6-7 (9 sessions total). Founded by Jon Kabat-Zinn, UMass Medical School, 1979.
- Home practice: 40-45 min RECORDED formal practice/day; guide's stated total 60+ min/day. (Common "45 min" shorthand undersells it.)
- Four formal practices: body scan, sitting meditation, yoga/mindful movement, walking meditation. Body scan is the FIRST practice assigned (wk 1-2, 6+ days/week); sitting builds 10-15 min (wk2) → 15-20 (wk3) → 20 (wk4); wk7 drops recordings, 45 min/day self-guided.
- Real-world adherence (Parsons 2017, 43 studies, N=1,427): 64% of assigned practice completed, ~30 min/day 6 days/week. Practice-outcome correlation r=0.26 (CI 0.19-0.34); later meta-regression found NO significant dose-response. "More = better" is weak and correlational.
- UMass curriculum guide is proprietary: paraphrase, never reproduce tables/scripts.

### Breath patterns (Session 2 - the pacer's four)
- 4-6 relaxing (longer exhale): gentlest default; exhale-emphasis consistent with Balban findings.
- Box 4-4-4-4: named + popularized by Mark Divine, former Navy SEAL commander (SEALFIT); pattern predates him (martial arts, pranayama). NOT verified as official Navy curriculum - say "popularized by a former SEAL commander".
- Coherent ~5.5: cardiovascular resonance near 0.1 Hz ≈ 6 breaths/min; individual resonance 4.5-7.0 bpm. Steffen 2017 (N=95): higher positive mood + lower systolic BP under stressor vs controls (p<0.05). Caveats: single session, young sample, contested LF/HF index.
- 4-7-8: Andrew Weil, 2010 handout, from pranayama; "natural tranquilizer" is HIS phrase not a finding; NO dedicated RCT for the ratio; Cleveland Clinic: reduces anxiety, does not "put you to sleep". Weakest evidence of the four - say so.
- Balban et al 2023, Cell Reports Medicine (111 participants, 4 arms, 5 min/day, ~28 days): cyclic sighing (exhale-focused) beat mindfulness meditation on mood + respiratory rate (p<0.05); ALL four arms improved; single unreplicated trial with a science-communicator senior author - scope claims to the trial.

### Traditions (Session 5 - one honest paragraph each)
- Vipassana (Goenka): Theravada roots via Ledi Sayadaw → U Ba Khin → Goenka (first course Rangoon 1955); 10-day silent residential format; 170+ centres; dana-funded (first-timers pay nothing). Caveat: intensive retreats are where adverse-effects literature flags elevated risk.
- Zen: Soto = shikantaza "just sitting" (Dogen, 1200-1253); Rinzai = koan practice (Hakuin, 1686-1768). "Gradual vs sudden" shorthand is useful but crude. Teacher-to-student religious transmission, not a technique package.
- TM: mantra-based, paid 1-on-1 instruction over 4 sessions, income-scaled ~$380-980. Research-quality critiques: reviews rated fair only (mean AMSTAR 5.75/11), risk of bias + conflict-of-interest concerns (much research from TM-affiliated institutions). Say "reviews rated only fair quality", not "doesn't work".
- Metta/loving-kindness: Galante 2014 meta-analysis - medium effects on positive emotions vs WAITLIST, small for compassion variants; effects shrink or vanish vs active controls.
- Secular mindfulness: Kabat-Zinn 1979, deliberately translated Zen/vipassana/yoga into clinical language; source of the definitional mess Van Dam flags.

### Adverse effects + cautions (Session 6)
- Britton et al 2021, Clinical Psychological Science (n=96, 44-item MedEx-I interview): 83% at least one meditation-related effect (any valence); 58% negative-valence adverse effects; 37% with negative impact on functioning; 6-14% lasting bad effects (6% beyond one month). Lasting harm tracked dysregulated arousal (hyperarousal, dissociation).
- Britton's own framing: rates similar to other psychological treatments. Not "meditation is dangerous", not "completely safe".
- Open-ended questioning underestimates adverse effects by ~70% vs specific private inquiry.
- Cautions (not rigid exclusions - adapted programs show promise): trauma history, psychosis-spectrum, severe depression, suicidality, addiction, recent crisis. Intensive retreats riskier than short daily practice. Not medical advice; if in treatment, loop in the provider.
- VCE study (Lindahl et al 2017, PLOS ONE): 100+ interviews, 26 influencing-factor categories across 4 domains - difficulty depends on person + context, not just technique.

### Habit + tools (Session 6)
- Lally et al 2010 (n=96): average 66 days to automaticity, RANGE 18-254; Lally: "highly variable". Never state 66 without the range.
- Apps (mid-2026, re-verify before quoting prices; prefer tier language): Headspace paid (~$70/yr), Calm paid (~$70/yr), Insight Timer big free tier (250k+ free tracks, open platform so quality uncontrolled), Waking Up paid but free scholarships to anyone who asks, Healthy Minds Program entirely free (donor-funded, RCT-tested - but flagship 2020 trial was waitlist-controlled, the exact design Van Dam criticizes).
- App efficacy meta-analyses: small effects (stress g=0.46, anxiety g=0.28, depression g=0.33 across 34 trials); not significantly different from active therapeutic comparisons.
- Free reputable guided: UCLA MARC / UCLA Mindful app (3-19 min, 18+ languages, free), Healthy Minds Program (free), Palouse Mindfulness (free full MBSR course by certified instructor Dave Potter, 501(c)(3); faithful but not UMass-authorized, no live instructor).
