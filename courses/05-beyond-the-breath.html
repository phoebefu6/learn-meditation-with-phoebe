Why this session exists

Breath-watching is the trunk of this course, and it stays the trunk. But some days the breath is the wrong tool - the inner critic is loud, or the body refuses to sit still - and some learners discover their real home is a different technique entirely. Part 1 · covers open awareness, metta, walking

Three techniques beyond the breath 8 min live

All three keep the same engine you built in Sessions 1-4: attention goes somewhere, wanders, gets noticed, comes back. What changes is the anchor - and each anchor turns out to be the right tool for a different kind of day.

LiveOpen awareness: the anchor is awareness itself3 min▶

In breath practice you pick one object and return to it. In open awareness (researchers call it open monitoring) you drop the fixed object: sounds, body sensations, thoughts and moods arise, get noticed, and pass - and you chase none of them. The instruction is almost nothing: sit, stay awake, let whatever shows up show up, and do not board any train of thought.

  • It is harder than it sounds. Without a home base, the mind boards trains constantly. That is why this course sequenced it here: it works best after about 2 weeks of anchor practice, when your notice-and-return reflex already fires on its own.
  • Traditionally, this is the heart of shikantaza - "just sitting" - the core practice of Soto Zen. Practitioners have traditionally said it cultivates a spacious, less reactive relationship with thought. Reported with respect; not a measured claim.
  • Practical use: a change of gear when breath-watching has gone stale, and a genuinely interesting way to watch your own mental weather.
LiveLoving-kindness (metta): phrases instead of breath3 min▶

Metta swaps the breath for silently repeated phrases of goodwill - classically aimed in widening circles: yourself, a friend, a neutral person, a difficult person, all beings. The phrases are the anchor; when the mind wanders, you come back to the phrase, same rep as always.

Research says: the Galante 2014 meta-analysis found medium effects on positive emotions - but versus waitlist controls (people doing nothing), small effects for compassion variants, and effects that shrink or vanish against active controls. Quoted honestly: metta reliably beats doing nothing; it has not been shown to beat other reasonable activities. Same shape as Session 1's evidence story.

  • It feels awkward at first. Repeating "may I be at ease" to yourself can feel forced, saccharine, or faintly ridiculous. That is normal, universal, and fine - the awkwardness fades with reps, and nobody can hear you.
  • Practical use: the days the inner critic is running the meeting. Aiming deliberate goodwill at yourself is a surprisingly direct counter-move.
Real world

The "difficult person" stage is optional for beginners. Classic instructions build up to sending goodwill to someone you find hard. Week one, skip it - self, friend, neutral person is plenty. The difficult person can wait until the phrases stop feeling like a costume.

LiveWalking meditation: the rescue technique2 min▶

Walking meditation puts the anchor in the feet: the contact of each step, the shift of weight, the swing of the leg. Slow pace or normal pace both work - slow gives you more detail per step, normal fits real corridors. Wander, notice, return to the feet.

  • Credentials: this is not a consolation prize for people who cannot sit. MBSR - the clinical program from Session 1 - includes walking as one of its four formal practices, alongside the body scan, sitting, and mindful movement.
  • Practical use: the rescue technique for restlessness days. Session 4 named restlessness as a classic obstacle; this is its dedicated counter. A hallway, ten steps long, is enough.
Self-studyWhy one engine, many anchors2 min read▶

Notice what did NOT change across the three techniques: the rep. Attention rests somewhere, drifts, gets caught, returns. Open awareness widens the anchor to everything; metta makes the anchor a phrase with emotional content; walking makes it kinesthetic. This is why your four weeks of breath practice transfer - you are not starting over, you are re-aiming a trained reflex.

It is also why this course resists technique-collecting. The rep deepens with repetition on a stable anchor; a new anchor every day means shallow reps on all of them. Part 3 turns this into a rule you can write down.

Part 2 · covers where this all came from

The honest tradition tour 7 min live

Everything in this course was developed inside contemplative traditions, then translated. You deserve the map: four lineages, one honest paragraph each - what they teach, what they cost, and what the research quality looks like. Respect without credulity; scrutiny without sneering.

LiveVipassana (Goenka): the 10-day deep end2 min▶

The Goenka school of vipassana has Theravada Buddhist roots, transmitted via Ledi Sayadaw to U Ba Khin to S.N. Goenka, who taught his first course in Rangoon in 1955. The format is famous and uncompromising: a 10-day silent residential retreat - no phone, no reading, no talking. There are 170+ centres worldwide, and the model is dana-funded: first-timers pay nothing, courses run on donations from past students. That funding model has earned genuine respect - nobody is upselling you at a Goenka retreat.

One honest caveat this course will not soften: intensive retreats are exactly where the adverse-effects literature flags elevated risk - long hours, high intensity, no gradual on-ramp. Session 6 covers that research properly. A 10-day silent retreat is a deep end, and deep ends are not where this course starts anyone.

LiveZen: two schools, one warning about shorthand2 min▶

Japanese Zen comes to most Westerners through two schools. Soto centres on shikantaza, "just sitting" - the open-awareness ancestor you tasted in Part 1 - articulated by Dogen (1200-1253). Rinzai centres on koan practice - riddles that resist analytical thinking - revitalized by Hakuin (1686-1768). The popular shorthand calls Soto "gradual" and Rinzai "sudden"; the shorthand is useful but crude, and Zen teachers wince at it.

The thing to understand honestly: Zen is a religious tradition transmitted teacher-to-student, not a technique package you can download. This course borrowed one exercise from its toolbox with attribution. Borrowing the exercise does not make anyone a Zen practitioner, and this course will not pretend otherwise.

LiveTM: the one with the price tag2 min▶

Transcendental Meditation is mantra-based: a teacher gives you a personal mantra in paid 1-on-1 instruction over 4 sessions, income-scaled at roughly $380-980. It is the only tradition on this tour that charges for the core instruction, which deserves a plain honest treatment rather than a sneer.

Research says: systematic reviews of TM's research have been rated only fair quality - mean AMSTAR score 5.75 out of 11 - with risk-of-bias and conflict-of-interest concerns, since much of the research comes from TM-affiliated institutions. Note the precise phrasing: "reviews rated only fair quality" is NOT the same claim as "TM doesn't work". It means the evidence is weaker and less independent than the marketing implies. Whether a mantra practice is worth $380-980 when breath-watching is free is a question you are now equipped to answer for yourself.

LiveSecular mindfulness (MBSR): the translation layer1 min▶

You met this one in Session 1: Jon Kabat-Zinn, 1979, UMass Medical School - a deliberate clinical translation of Zen, vipassana and yoga into secular, insurable, studyable language. It is the reason this course can exist: the translation made the practices available without doctrinal commitments.

It is also the source of the definitional mess Van Dam flagged in the "Mind the Hype" paper - once "mindfulness" named a trait, a state, a technique and an 8-week program all at once, the research became hard to compare. The translation gave us access AND gave us the hype problem. Both things are true; this course uses the access and corrects for the hype.

Self-studyHow to visit a tradition without joining it2 min read▶

If one of these called to you, the respectful move is to go to the source: sit with a real Zen group, read Goenka's own talks, take the actual TM course if the price feels fair to you. The traditions are not theme parks, and their teachers know more about their practices than any secular summary can carry.

The honest move, meanwhile, is to keep your three labels with you. Every tradition contains: practices (testable), traditional claims (respectable, unmeasured), and modern marketing (ignorable). The labels travel. Use them everywhere.

Part 3 · covers the mixing principle

Picking your mix 4 min live

Four techniques is a menu, not a to-do list. The principle for weeks 3-4 of your plan is deliberately boring: one main technique, one alternate.

LiveOne main, one alternate - and why not rotation3 min▶
  • Your MAIN is what you do most days. For most people that stays the breath - it is the simplest anchor and every study dose you have met was built on repetition of one practice.
  • Your ALTERNATE is for named days. Metta for the days the inner critic is loud; walking for the days the body will not sit. Naming the trigger day in advance means no decision-making at practice time - Session 4 taught you why that matters.
  • Do not rotate daily. A different technique every day feels like variety and delivers shallowness: too little time on each anchor to build the rep. Rotation is the technique-collector's version of never going to the same gym twice.
  • Changing the mix at week 4 is expected, not failure. The week-4 review exists precisely so you can swap the alternate, or promote it to main, with data instead of guilt. A plan you adjust is a plan that is working.
Practice 1 of 2

Taste all three ✦ 12 min · everyone does this

You cannot pick a technique from a description. Twelve minutes, three tastings, four minutes each. Sit however Session 3 taught you; a hallway or kitchen handles the walking leg.

4 min · open awareness. Sit, no fixed anchor. Let sounds, sensations and thoughts arise and pass. When you notice you have boarded a train of thought, step off and return to open watching. Expect more trains than usual - that is the exercise, not a malfunction.

4 min · metta. Silently repeat, at an unhurried pace: "may I be at ease" for two minutes, then picture a friend and repeat "may you be at ease" for two. When the mind wanders, return to the phrase. If it feels awkward, you are doing it correctly.

4 min · walking. Walk slowly - to the kitchen and back counts. Attention on foot contact: heel, weight, push-off. Mind wanders, return to the feet. Ten slow steps, turn, repeat.

30 seconds · verdict. Which one would you actually reach for on a bad day? That instinct is data for plan step 5, right now.

Real world

Typical tasting results: open awareness feels either fascinating or like static; metta splits the room between "surprisingly moving" and "I felt like a greeting card"; walking is the sleeper hit for the restless. There is no correct reaction - there is only your reaction, which is the point of tasting.

Practice 2 of 2 · 🧩 plan step 5

Write your technique mix ✦ 5 min · plan step 5 of 6

Steps 1-4 gave your plan a why, an anchor and slot, a posture and duration ladder, and an obstacle playbook. Step 5 sets the technique mix for weeks 3-4 - one main, one alternate, trigger days named in advance.

Pick your main. The technique you will do most days. Breath is the default recommendation; override it only if today's tasting gave you a strong signal.

Pick your alternate and name its day. Not "sometimes metta" but "metta on days the self-talk turns harsh" or "walking on days I cannot sit still". The trigger does the deciding so you do not have to.

Pre-authorize the week-4 swap. Write the review date down. Adjusting the mix there is the plan working as designed.
